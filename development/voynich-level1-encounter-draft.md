# The Voynich Codex: The Inkbound Threshold
## Level 1 private playtest encounter draft — NOT FOR PUBLICATION OR SALE

**Format:** 60–90 minute tabletop session for four level-1 characters and one Dungeon Master (DM). Original fantasy scenario; not a claim to reproduce or translate the historical Voynich manuscript. This draft has no commissioned maps, finished layout, or tested balancing. Use only for internal development.

### Adventure hook
A village archivist, Mara Fen, asks the party to recover a missing field journal from the abandoned Glasshouse Scriptorium before rain floods the cellar. She offers 20 gold pieces for its safe return. The journal records the location of a healing spring, but its final page is sealed with an unfamiliar botanical clue.

### Scene 1 — The broken gate (exploration)
Read aloud: *The glasshouse roof is a jagged crown against the evening sky. Water drips through shattered panes. Beneath the ivy, a brass plate reads: “Only what grows may pass.”*

A stone gate has three carved emblems: a seed, a flame, and a key. Nearby, three ceramic pots contain dry soil. Planting a seed from the pouch on the gardener's hook opens the gate in one minute. A character who investigates the plate can infer the solution with a DC 10 Intelligence (Investigation) check. Failure never blocks progress: a careful search finds the pouch and a faded gardener's note. Breaking the rusted side latch instead requires a DC 12 Strength (Athletics) check and makes noise. On a failed check, the latch still yields after a noisy second attempt that takes one extra minute; failure changes the situation but never traps the party at the gate.

### Scene 2 — The rootbound hall (hazard and choice)
Read aloud: *Pale roots cross the tiled floor like handwriting. A silver watering can sits beside a narrow channel carrying rainwater.*

The first creature to rush across without checking the floor must succeed on a DC 11 Dexterity saving throw or fall prone. The roots are not hostile. Filling the watering can and pouring water into the dry trough causes the roots to withdraw, revealing a safe path and a brass leaf token. A character can instead cross carefully at half speed. The token grants advantage on one check to interpret the botanical clue in the recovered journal.

### Scene 3 — The cellar keeper (social or combat)
A frightened animated scarecrow guards the journal, believing it protects the greenhouse from thieves. It can be persuaded to stand down by returning the brass leaf token to its chest or by succeeding on a DC 11 Charisma (Persuasion) check after explaining the archivist's request. If combat occurs, use a **single crawling claw** stat block from a legally available rules source as a temporary playtest stand-in, described as a root-wrapped glove; do not reproduce third-party stat block text in the final package. The DM should review encounter difficulty and available legal rules before testing. The guardian ceases fighting if the journal is left undamaged.

### The journal and resolution
The journal contains a sketch of a five-petaled flower and a botanical clue. The inscription reads: **PETALS POINT TO WATER**. A DC 10 Intelligence (Investigation) check reveals that the five-petaled sketch matches the village fountain. The party can solve it without rolling by comparing the flower to the fountain emblem described by Mara. Returning the journal earns 20 gold pieces and a future lead to the spring. Taking it to the fountain reveals a sealed stone compartment containing an empty glass vial and a note: “The spring wakes at first frost.”

### Dungeon Master (DM) map brief — production reference, not finished artwork
Prepare two separate original maps. **Player map:** the glasshouse gate, three soil pots, rootbound hall, cellar door, and village fountain; omit hidden mechanisms and the keeper's intentions. **DM map:** the same spaces with numbered keyed locations (1 gate and seed pouch; 2 root hazard and watering trough; 3 cellar keeper and journal; 4 village fountain compartment). Mark the alternative side-latch entrance and all clue locations. Ensure labels remain readable at print size and never reveal the fountain secret on the player version.

### Dungeon Master (DM) encounter safety and pacing
The guardian should first ask why the characters have entered; give the party an obvious opportunity to talk or return the leaf token before initiative. The root hazard causes a fall, not automatic damage. If a player misses a clue, introduce the gardener's note rather than blocking the story. This is a preliminary playtest plan, not a verified encounter balance claim.

### DM playtest observation prompts
- Did players find the seed solution without getting stuck?
- Was the root hazard telegraphed clearly enough to feel fair?
- Did the keeper offer a meaningful noncombat resolution?
- Did all four characters have an opportunity to contribute?
- Was the ending satisfying and did the fountain hook motivate a next session?
